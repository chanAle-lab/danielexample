The Four Musical Clefs & Middle C

ABRSM progresses through four clefs from Grade 1 to Grade 5. Notice how Middle C (C4) is notated across each clef:

1. Treble Clef (G Clef) - Grade 1+

Curls around line 2 (G4). Middle C sits on the first ledger line below the stave.

2. Bass Clef (F Clef) - Grade 1+

Dots enclose line 4 (F3). Middle C sits on the first ledger line above the stave.

3. Alto Clef (C Clef) - Grade 4+

Center arrow points directly to line 3 (middle line) which is Middle C (C4). Standard clef for the Viola.

4. Tenor Clef (C Clef) - Grade 5+

Center arrow points to line 4 which is Middle C (C4). Used for upper registers of Cello, Bassoon, and Trombone.

ABRSM Cadences & Chord Inversions (Grade 5)

In Grade 5, candidates identify Perfect, Plagal, and Imperfect cadences, and chord inversions: Root position (a / 5-3), First inversion (b / 6-3), and Second inversion (c / 6-4).

ABRSM Inversion Symbols (a, b, c):

Inversion Symbol Bass Note Intervals Above Bass Example (C Major)
Root Position a (or 5/3) Root (C) 3rd and 5th C in bass (C - E - G)
First Inversion b (or 6/3 / 6) Third (E) 3rd and 6th E in bass (E - G - C)
Second Inversion c (or 6/4) Fifth (G) 4th and 6th G in bass (G - C - E)

Standard Orchestral Instruments & Transposition (Grades 4 & 5)

ABRSM questions test instrument families, sound generation mechanisms, standard clefs, and transposition to/from concert pitch for instruments in B♭, A, and F.

Master Transposition Rule:
  • Instrument in B♭ (Clarinet in B♭, Trumpet in B♭): Sounds a Major 2nd lower than written. (To write for it: transpose UP a Major 2nd).
  • Instrument in A (Clarinet in A): Sounds a Minor 3rd lower than written. (To write for it: transpose UP a Minor 3rd).
  • Instrument in F (French Horn in F, Cor Anglais): Sounds a Perfect 5th lower than written. (To write for it: transpose UP a Perfect 5th).
