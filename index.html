The Four Clefs & Middle C

How pitches map across the four clefs required across ABRSM Grades 1 to 5.

1. Treble Clef (G Clef)

Grade 1+

The loop curls around line 2 (G4). Middle C (C4) sits on the first ledger line below the stave.

2. Bass Clef (F Clef)

Grade 1+

The two dots enclose line 4 (F3). Middle C (C4) sits on the first ledger line above the stave.

3. Alto Clef (C Clef)

Grade 4+

The center notch points precisely to line 3 (middle line) as Middle C (C4). The standard clef for the Viola.

4. Tenor Clef (C Clef)

Grade 5+

The center notch points to line 4 as Middle C (C4). Used for the high registers of Cello, Bassoon, and Trombone.

Orchestral Instruments & Transposition

Standard instrument families, sound mechanisms, clefs, and transposition rules in B♭, A, and F.

Grade 4 & 5
Master Transposition Rules:
  • Instrument in B♭ (Clarinet, Trumpet): Sounds a Major 2nd lower than written. (To write for it: transpose UP a Major 2nd).
  • Instrument in A (Clarinet in A): Sounds a Minor 3rd lower than written. (To write for it: transpose UP a Minor 3rd).
  • Instrument in F (French Horn, Cor Anglais): Sounds a Perfect 5th lower than written. (To write for it: transpose UP a Perfect 5th).
